# SpinSmith: why it feels cheap (game feel, juice, VFX, the tops as hero objects)

Read-only audit, 2026-10-08. Sources: `src/game/visuals.ts`, `match-system.ts`, `audio.ts`, `room-light.ts`, `comfort.ts`, `hands.ts`, `sim.ts`, `parts.ts`, `reach.ts`; screenshots `docs/night/design/r4-fight.png`, `r2-fight.png`, `r2-result.png`, `r3-win-pillar.png`, `vr-fight.png`. The grey room and the hands come from the IWER emulator (IWSDK's browser XR emulator), not from the game.

## Summary

- **The skeleton is there. The flesh is thin.** The game already has a hit-stop that pauses only the sim (70-110 ms, `match-system.ts:3170`), a 0.25 s super freeze on a strike (`:2926`), a knockout slow-mo, a replay, a rival entrance and a win pillar. Almost every one of these is rendered with 1-pixel lines, thin flat rings or 80-180 ms flashes, so each beat is *registered* but never *felt*.
- **The sparks are WebGL `LineSegments`, which are always 1 px wide on Quest** (`visuals.ts:1513`). At 0.5-0.9 m they look like scratches or hair (r4-fight, r2-fight, r3-win-pillar). Nothing in the effect has volume, glow or embers that linger. This is the single biggest "cheap" signal.
- **The tops do not read as screaming, dangerous hero objects.** The drawn spin is at most 28 rad/s, about 4.5 rev/s (`sim.ts:734`), so they turn like a lazy turntable. The streak halo turns at 0.12x that (`visuals.ts:705`). The blades share one grey brushed metal (`visuals.ts:139`), so silhouettes are low, flat discs about 12-14 cm across. In the screenshots they fill about 5-8% of the frame width, and the arena dominates them.
- **Impacts have no weight on the objects themselves.** There is no scale punch, no emissive bloom on the blades, no knockback squash, no floor shockwave on normal hard hits (only a 0.18 s thin ring, `visuals.ts:1849`), and no lingering mark. A light hit (strength < 0.6) gets only 4-16 hairline sparks and a clang. Parry has no hit-stop and no sparks (`match-system.ts:3126-3132`).
- **Knockouts and wins are flat and fragile.** A spin-out gets a 4-note clatter and a 0.45 s fall, with no slow-mo and no sparks (`:3231`, `:3288`). The replay is silent and stiff: no sparks, no sounds, no lean, and spin is forced to 1 (`:1368-1378`). The win pillar lasts 1.2 s and is turned off entirely in the Calm comfort modes (`:3261-3266`) with nothing to replace it. There is no haptic feedback at all, not even on controllers (`hands.ts:95` reads buttons and never pulses).

## Hero tops assessment

- **Size.** The ring collision radius is 0.024-0.030 m (`parts.ts:65-165`), drawn 2.4x (`reach.ts:17`). That gives a world diameter of about 11.5-14.5 cm and a height of about 5 cm (tip 4-7 mm, core 5-8 mm, ring 6-10 mm, times 2.4). The arena center is about 0.68 m ahead (`placement.ts:10`), so a top spans about 10-12° when seen from a seated, downward angle. In the screenshots a top is about 80-110 px of a 2000 px frame, against an arena of about 650 px. The arena reads as the hero and the tops as pieces on it.
- **Silhouette.** Every ring is a thin extruded outline (blade 3.2-8 mm sim, `visuals.ts:369-416`) under a small dome. From a 30-45° down angle all of them read as a flat disc with a nub. The distinctive outlines (striker swords, hook claws, cog teeth, saw) are only legible from above and at rest. In motion (r4-fight) the two tops merge into one grey blob with an orange and a cyan smear.
- **Differentiation.** Both tops share `BLADE_METAL` (grey brushed steel). Team identity is carried by the thin colored layer (2.5 mm), the emblem chip, the floor team ring and the halo tint. That works at a glance thanks to the orange and cyan rings, but it is UI-like identity, not object identity.
- **Detail.** There is respectable modelling detail: bevels, inlay line, bolts, emblem texture, brushed roughness map. But it is sub-centimetre and lost at distance, and the IWER render has no bloom. The emissive emblem is `MeshBasicMaterial` with no glow spill.
- **How spinning is shown.** The real spinner rotation is capped at about 4.5 rev/s, and the streak ring sweeps at about 0.5 rev/s. The parts stay crisp at all speeds, so the eye sees "a toy turning slowly with a translucent ring on it", not "a blade at 6000 rpm". There is no speed-dependent motion-blur disc, no spark-scrape at the tip, and no dust or heat shimmer.
- **Parts visible?** Yes, and too clearly: at full spin the blade is as sharp as at rest. The exploded "ring lifting off" at high damage (`visuals.ts:721`) is a great idea, but it is a 0.35x offset of about 4 mm and is barely visible.
- **Contact.** A soft shadow plane at opacity 0.5 grounds them well (good). There is no tip contact glow or floor scuffing.

## Why it feels cheap

1. **Hairline particles.** `Sparks` uses `LineSegments` + `LineBasicMaterial`. WebGL ignores `linewidth`, so on Quest each spark is a 1 px line, about 0.1 mm at the viewing distance. The tail is `0.025*vel` long and life is 0.25-0.55 s. The "showers" in r3-win-pillar look like scratched glass, not molten sparks. There are no sprite embers, no glow, no smoke puff and no lingering hot dots on the floor.
2. **No mass on contact.** On a hard hit the tops only quiver ±2 mm (`shake`) and flash a translucent halo for 80 ms. Nothing scales, squashes, recoils visibly, glows hot or leaves a mark. The impact ring is a thin 0.8-1.0 annulus, 0.18 s long, max 14 cm. The flare is 0.12 s and only above strength 0.8. Every cue is shorter than a VR player's attention shift (about 150-250 ms).
3. **The hit-stop is invisible.** It is correct and outside the sim (`updateFight` returns early, `:3111`). But sparks, impact rings and trails keep running at real dt (`:976-987`), and the top's halo keeps rendering. With no accent (a scale pop at freeze start, a brighter frame, an audio transient), 70 ms just reads as a dropped frame.
4. **The launch has no anticipation or payoff.** The pull charge is good (cord heat, tone, charge ring). The release, though, is a hiss, a 0.35 s stamp and a small burst at the spawn point. There is no "snap" (cord whip and recoil, a top drop-and-bite with a floor ring and dust), and the rival launches the same frame, so the two first contacts blur together.
5. **The tops spin too slowly and are too crisp to look dangerous.** See above. The hum and whirr pitch track spin well, but the visuals do not ramp in intensity: no glow at full spin and no visible spin-down drama besides a slight lean.
6. **Knockouts are uneven.** Burst and ring-out are decent physically (a real-table landing is a great MR beat). But `playOver` is one hiss. Spin-out, the most common finish (`outlastReason` exists because it "reads as luck", `:3274`), gets almost nothing. The slow-mo is visual-only and stops the stamp from punching harder. The KO itself has no freeze frame.
7. **The replay deflates the moment.** Ghosts have no velocity or kick (no lean), spin is forced to 1, and no clash sparks or sounds are re-emitted. A silent, stiff 5 s re-run makes the knockout feel *less* impactful the second time.
8. **The win celebration is short and comfort-fragile.** The pillar lasts 1.2 s at 29 cm, and the rim sparks are hairlines. With Calm/Assist modes (`comfort.ts:21-24`) the pillar, room wave, flare, halo flash and room hit flash all disappear with no non-flashing substitute. The win then is a stamp and a jingle.
9. **No haptics and no hand-side feedback.** Your hands are the controller, but nothing ever happens *on* them: no glow when the strike lands, no buzz on a clash with controllers, no "recoil" on the cord hand at release.
10. **Synth audio is thin in places.** `playLaunch` and `playOver` are filtered noise only, and `playTopple` is 4 soft ticks. The clash synthesis is good, but there is no low-end "thump" layer on heavy hits beyond a 180 Hz sine at 0.07 s.
11. **A stale constant suggests tuning drift.** `HIT_STOP = 0.045` (`:108`) and its "45 ms" comment (`:95`) are unused. The real values are 70/110 ms at `:3170`.

## Fix plan ranked

Determinism rule: `src/game/sim.ts` (checked by `npm run check:sim`) must not change. All fixes below live in `visuals.ts`, `match-system.ts` presentation code, `audio.ts`, or new view-side state. Hit-stop and slow-mo already pause or scale *wall-clock feeding* of the fixed-step sim and never alter `step()` inputs. Keep it that way: never scale `STEP` or pass a scaled dt into `step()`. The existing `accumulator = 0` on a freeze is safe because the sim advances only in whole `STEP`s.
