# Why SpinSmith feels cheap: materials, lighting, rendering, grounding

Scope: the game's own objects only (tops, arena bowl and shell, anvil, pedestals, sign slabs, tray). The grey low-poly room and grey hands in the screenshots are IWER's synthetic passthrough and are ignored.
Screenshots reviewed: `docs/night/design/r4-home.png`, `r4-fight.png`, `r4-forge.png`, `r4-workshop.png`, `r3-win-pillar.png`, `r2-result.png`.

## Summary

- **About half the hero surfaces are matcaps, not lit materials.** The anvil, the stands, the arena walls, pillars and floor, the top's tip and chip dome, and the workshop props all use `MeshMatcapMaterial` with a 128x128 matcap generated in code (`visuals.ts:64-127`, `hub.ts:91-97`). A matcap's lighting is fixed to the camera, so in VR the highlights move with your head instead of staying on the object. They also ignore the env map, so these surfaces sit next to PBR parts that are lit from other directions. That mix is the single biggest reason things read as toy or clay.
- **No surface has micro-detail except the top blades and the arena floor's color map.** Only one `roughnessMap` exists in the whole codebase (`BLADE_METAL`, `visuals.ts:139`). There are no normal maps, no AO maps, and no roughness variation anywhere else. The shell, plinth, ratchet, sign slabs and anvil are one flat value each, so each reads as injection-moulded plastic.
- **The lighting is a single static, generic env map with no key light and no focal pool on the objects.** There are zero `Light` objects (`index.ts:36` sets only `scene.environment`). Tone mapping is left at three's default `NoToneMapping`, while the env emitters are 4-6x over white (`visuals.ts:2269-2274`), so chrome highlights clip hard. The arena gets exactly the same light as a sign 50 cm away. The "pool of light" in `room-light.ts` only dims the passthrough; it never brightens the arena.
- **The palette is mostly dark greys sitting a few hex steps apart, with no hierarchy.** The arena floor and the shell share the exact color `0x5b5f69` (`visuals.ts:940` vs `forge-arena-shell.scene-asset.ts:20`). On top of that come `0x41444b`, `0x4a4e57`, `0x3a3d45`, `0x2b2f37`, `0x26282d`, and every one of them is mid-rough, semi-metal. In `r4-fight` the bowl, collar and plinth merge into one muddy brown-grey puck, made worse by the warm softbox reflecting in metalness 0.45.
- **Grounding is half done.** Blob shadows exist (arena, tops, anvil, stands), but there is no AO where things meet: wall foot to floor, collar to plinth, part to tray pad, slab stem to table. There are no bevels on box parts (clamps, anvil face, prongs). Emissive "heat" is unlit MeshBasic lines that cast no light on their surroundings. The one moment that looks premium is the additive warm column in `r3-win-pillar`, and it is premium precisely because it adds light and atmosphere around the arena.

## Lighting and rendering setup

- **Renderer** (IWSDK `world-initializer.js:245-253`): `antialias: true`, `multiviewStereo: true`, alpha in AR, and `outputColorSpace = SRGBColorSpace`. **`toneMapping` is not set** (three's default is `NoToneMapping`), and the scene JSON (`public/scenes/main.iwsdk.scene.json`) has no environment block. The game also sets fixed foveation to 0.5 (`index.ts:37`).
- **Lights:** none. No Directional, Spot, Point or Hemisphere light anywhere in `src/`. IWSDK's light components are registered but unused. Shadow maps are off.
- **Env map** (`visuals.ts:2260-2279`, assigned at `index.ts:36`): a PMREM of a small box scene with a grey background `0x8e9198`, a warm softbox low-left (x4), a cool strip behind (x3), a white overhead line (x6), a ceiling panel (x2.5), an ember bounce on the right (x1.2), and a dark floor below. It is built once, `sigma 0.04`, and is static. `environmentIntensity` and `envMapIntensity` are never tuned (all 1.0). Being static is fine. The problems are:
  1. With no tone mapping, the x4-x6 emitters clip to flat white or yellow on metal 1.0 / rough 0.28-0.34 surfaces, giving "plastic chrome" with no roll-off.
  2. The grey background dominates the diffuse irradiance, so every non-metal gets the same flat, frontal, shadowless grey fill. Nothing is darker underneath than on top.
  3. The warm softbox reflected in the large semi-metal shell (metal 0.45) is what turns the graphite plinth brown or mauve in `r4-fight` and `r2-result`.
- **Matcaps** (`visuals.ts:73-127`): light vector `(-0.5, 0.7, 0.6)` in *view* space. The clay ramp runs 0.42-1.12, so no surface ever goes darker than 42% of its base color: low contrast, no occlusion. Metal and chrome are one hard-coded studio strip.
- **RoomLight** (`room-light.ts`): a black back-face sphere around the head that dims passthrough outside a radius around the arena (TUNE.dim = 1). This makes the real room darker, but it adds no light to the virtual objects. Virtual objects inside and outside the pool are shaded identically.
- **Grounding helpers present:** SOFT_SHADOW blobs (`visuals.ts:190-207`), used for tops (619), arena (1318 wide + 1322 tight contact), anvil and stand (`hub.ts:691-696`), plus match-system scorches and palm prints.

## Why it looks cheap (evidence)

1. **View-locked matcap highlights on the showpieces.** The anvil (r4-home right, r4-forge center) has a chrome face whose bright strip sits at a fixed screen-relative position (`visuals.ts:93-103`). When the head moves, the highlight slides with it and the anvil looks like a sticker of an anvil. The same happens to the arena wall ring (`visuals.ts:1204`): in r4-fight and r2-result the white rim line has the same brightness all the way round, front and back, which no real metal ring under real light does. The emulator stills cannot show the motion, but in the headset it is the strongest "fake" cue, because parallax and head motion are exactly what VR adds.
2. **Two lighting models in one frame.** Matcap parts are lit from the viewer's upper-left. PBR parts are lit by the env's warm-left and cool-back boxes in world space. The top shows both at once: matcap tip and dome over a PBR blade and ratchet. The anvil (matcap) stands next to the shell (PBR). The eye reads the inconsistency as "assets from different kits".
3. **Uniform roughness, zero micro-detail on large surfaces.** The forge shell lathe (`forge-arena-shell:33-49`) is ~35-40% of the r4-fight frame, and it is one value of `0x5b5f69 / 0.45 / 0.5` with no normal or roughness map. It reads as a rubber tyre or plastic puck. Sign slabs (0.28 rough, `ui.ts:196`) look the same: a glossy, even, toy-like finish.
4. **The floor is printed, not machined.** FLOOR_TEXTURE (`visuals.ts:955-1031`) carries spun brushing and engraved grooves in the albedo only, under a clay matcap (`visuals.ts:1182`). The grooves never catch a specular glint and the brushing never shimmers as you move. In r4-home and r2-result the bowl reads as a vinyl record print.
5. **Greys without hierarchy.** Floor `0x5b5f69` equals shell iron `0x5b5f69`. Collar `0x41444b`, graphite `0x4a4e57`, plinth `0x3a3d45`, slab `0x2b2f37`, trough `0x26282d`, ratchet `0x6b717d`. Seven greys within ~0x45 of each other, all mid-rough and semi-metal. Nothing tells the eye "this is the important bright metal, this is the dark machine body, this is trim". In r4-fight the bowl edge, collar and plinth blend into one dark mass and the tops (the actual heroes, ~2-3 cm) are lost in it.
6. **No focal light on the arena.** Every object gets identical irradiance (static env, no lights). The arena floor is not brighter than the table props, and the tops are not brighter than the floor. Compare r3-win-pillar: the warm additive column and lit floor immediately make the scene look expensive. That is what is missing during normal play.
7. **Clipped highlights, no tone mapping.** Env emitters at 4-6x with `NoToneMapping` mean blade, bezel and anvil-face highlights hard-clip, and the warm box clips toward yellow-white. Clipped flat highlights are the classic cheap-CG cue.
8. **No contact occlusion where parts meet.** There is no darkening at the wall foot inside the bowl (`visuals.ts:1222-1224` walls just sit on the floor), none under the collar torus, around clamps, at the slab stem foot (`ui.ts:250-253`), or under the forge parts on the tray pads (`forge.ts:835`, r4-forge: parts look pasted onto discs). The clay matcap's 0.42 floor means crevices never get dark either.
9. **Sharp primitives with no bevels.** Shell clamps and shoulders (`forge-arena-shell:63-65`), the anvil face and hardy (`hub.ts:594-596`), stand prongs (`hub.ts:683`), the cradle (`hub.ts:414`) and forge tool icons (`forge.ts:889-911`) are raw `BoxGeometry`. Real machined parts always have a chamfer that catches a thin highlight line. Without one, edges alias and look like CAD blockout. (The top blades and anvil body do bevel: `visuals.ts:441`, `hub.ts:633`.)
10. **The fake fresnel on every sign** (`ui.ts:197-204`): a constant warm rim of 0.55 on all edges whatever the light direction, so a dozen signs all glow orange at their borders. It reads as a UI theme, not material, and adds to the "mobile game skin" feel.
11. **Heat is unlit lines.** Orange seams and rims are thin MeshBasic tubes (1.1-1.5 mm; `visuals.ts:1315`, `hub.ts:590`) that emit nothing onto neighboring surfaces. An "ember" that does not warm the iron next to it reads as a painted stripe.
12. **Blob shadows have no light direction.** The top shadow is centered under the top (`visuals.ts:619-622`), and with no key light there is no direction to cast it in. It grounds, but it gives no sense of a light above the arena.

## Fix plan, ranked by impact / effort

Perf budget context: Quest 3, multiview, 72-90 fps, no postprocessing. The current scene is light (a few dozen draw calls after `mergeByMaterial`), so there is headroom for per-fragment PBR on hero objects. The risk is fill rate on large surfaces (shell, floor, signs), not vertex count.
